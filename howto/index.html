Operating manual FS-4

How to use the Vibralizer

Play one long note into your microphone. The Vibralizer records it, shows how wide and how fast your vibrato is, how steady the volume was and whether the note was in tune, then plays it back so you can hear it. Compare yourself with great players, or with your own earlier notes.

01 Quick start

  1. Switch it on with On / Off (or Space) and allow the microphone when your browser asks.
  2. Pick your instrument with the keys at the bottom: violin, viola, cello, flute or trumpet.
  3. Wait for the blinking line. When the display says “● Play a note to record”, it is listening.
  4. Play one long note with your vibrato, up to 5 seconds, and let it fade away.
  5. Watch and listen. The graphs appear, are refined by a sweeping needle, and your note plays back by itself.
  6. Compare. Move the Recordings slider to another slot and press Play to hear a master, or one of your earlier notes.

02 The display

The backlit screen shows the last note: the note itself on the left, two graphs over its length on the right. While the Vibralizer is off and nothing is playing, the screen stays dark.

← Swipe to see the whole display →

  1. Tuning scale. From ♭ (bottom) to ♯ (top). The arrow marks the note’s average pitch, the frame shows how far the vibrato swung either side of it.
  2. The note and its offset in cents (a hundredth of a semitone), e.g. “+8 ¢ sharp”; within ±5 cents it says “In tune”.
  3. The written note in your instrument’s clef. Viola and cello switch to treble clef for high notes; the trumpet is shown at written pitch in B♭.
  4. Pitch graph. Your pitch over the length of the note, in cents from the nearest note. The background turns from green (in tune) to red. The scale grows (±25 to ±200 cents) to fit wide vibratos.
  5. Dashed line: the note’s average pitch; the solid line is the exact note.
  6. Vibrato width and rate: how far the pitch swings, peak to peak in cents, and how many swings per second. “No vibrato” if it hardly moved.
  7. Ready to record: blinks while the Vibralizer listens for your next note; “● REC” while a note is being recorded.
  8. Level graph. How loud the note was over its length, in dB. A steady bow or breath gives a flat line; the vibrato shows as a small ripple.
  9. Range: how far the volume swings with the vibrato, peak to peak in dB. A crescendo or fade over the whole note doesn’t count.

When a note has ended, a needle sweeps across the graphs and draws a more precise analysis over the first, live one (which stays faintly underneath). While a recording plays back, a needle follows the sound across the graphs.

03 Keys & knobs

Knobs: drag up or down (hold Shift for fine steps), scroll, or use the arrow and Page keys when selected. Double-click a knob to reset it.

On / Off

Switches the Vibralizer on and off. Off stops the tone and any recording playing, darkens the display, and disables every other key and knob until you switch on again.

Input

Microphone gain, −12 to +24 dB. Turn it up if quiet notes aren’t recorded, down if the input LEDs hit the top.

440
Ref. A4

The reference pitch, 420–460 Hz. Click to type a value, drag or scroll to change it, double-click for 440 Hz.

  • Left LEDs: the input level.
  • Right LEDs: the tune lamp. Sharp above, flat below, the middle lit when in tune, green to red with the offset.
Tone

Plays the reference A4 as a steady tone to tune to. Press again to stop. Nothing is recorded while it plays, so the tone itself never ends up as a note.

Volume

How loud everything plays, 0–100 % (100 % at first; double-click to reset): the reference tone, your notes and the recordings. The analysis always hears them at full level.

04 Recordings

Every instrument has five slots. A slot holds a recording of a great player, or a note of yours, or nothing (“No recording”).

Slider

Picks a slot. Click, drag, scroll or use the arrow keys. The display shows that slot’s graphs at once, or turns empty.

YOU 14:32
Readout

Names what the slot holds. Click your own note’s name to rename it; Enter keeps the new name, Esc the old one.

Play

Plays the slot (and turns into Stop), with its graphs and a needle following the sound. The microphone is muted meanwhile.

Reverb

Adds a concert-hall reverb to what plays back, 0–100 % (off at first). The analysis always hears the dry sound.

Where your notes go

Each note you play is kept automatically in the slot selected while you play it, named “You” and the time, and replaces what was there. So pick an empty slot (or one of your own) before playing if you want to keep a master’s recording. Your notes are kept until you reload the page; the masters come back then.

For the violin, slot 1 is empty and slots 2–5 hold Jascha Heifetz, David Oistrakh, Philippe Hirschhorn and Maxim Vengerov.

05 Instruments & tuner

Violin · Viola · Cello · Flute · Trumpet

Sets the clef and the pitch range the Vibralizer listens in, so sounds outside your instrument’s range are ignored. Switching clears the display.

Tuner

Turns the display into a tuner: a needle over ±50 cents, the note, its frequency and the offset. It starts chromatic (any note). An instrument key tunes that instrument’s open strings (violin G D A E, viola and cello C G D A) or chromatically (flute, trumpet in B♭); press it again for chromatic. Press Tuner again to go back.

06 How a note is recorded

You never press record: the Vibralizer listens and catches each note by itself.

  • Start. A note starts when the sound is loud enough (−42 dBFS) with a clear pitch in your instrument’s range. The recording reaches back to the first moment that pitch could be heard, so the attack is included but the room noise before it isn’t.
  • Stop. It ends when the sound falls well below its loudest point (30 dB, but never lower than −52 dBFS) for a moment, so a bow change doesn’t end it, or after 0.3 s without any pitch.
  • Any pitch movement is fine. However wide your vibrato or slide, the note goes on until the sound fades.
  • Up to 5 seconds. A longer note is cut there; holding it on doesn’t start a new recording until you stop or change note.
  • Too short (under 0.35 s): ignored, so a knock or cough doesn’t replace your last note.
  • Afterwards the note is analysed again from its sound, more precisely, and plays back by itself. For a moment after any playback the microphone ignores the room, so the speakers’ echo isn’t recorded as a new note.

07 What the numbers mean

Vibrato width (¢)

How far the pitch swings, from the lowest to the highest point of a swing, in cents. 100 cents are a semitone. Many string players use 20–60 cents; a slow drift of the pitch doesn’t count.

Rate (Hz)

Swings per second, where the swing best repeats itself (3–10 Hz). Typical vibratos are around 5–7 Hz.

Offset (¢)

The average pitch over the held note (without its start and end), against the nearest note at your reference pitch.

Range (dB)

How much the volume rises and falls with each vibrato swing. Only shown when there is a steady vibrato.

09 Questions

Nothing is recorded

Check that On / Off is lit and the input LEDs move when you play. If they barely move, turn Input up. Make sure the right instrument is selected: notes outside its range are ignored. Nothing is recorded while a recording plays back or in tuner mode.

The browser doesn’t ask for the microphone, or it was refused

The display then says “Microphone access denied” or “No microphone found”. Allow the microphone for this site in your browser’s settings (the icon left of the address) and switch on again.

My note is cut short, or split

A note ends when the sound drops away for a moment or after 5 seconds. Play into the microphone from a steady distance, and keep notes under 5 seconds.

I recorded over Heifetz!

Your notes go into the selected slot. Reload the page and the masters are back (your own notes are gone then). To keep both, choose an empty slot before you play.

Can I use it offline, like an app?

Yes. Install it from your browser (“Add to Home Screen” on iPhone and iPad, “Install app” in Chrome and Edge). Recordings you have played once are kept for offline use.

Headphones or speakers?

Either. The microphone is muted while a recording plays and for a moment after, so the speakers aren’t recorded.
